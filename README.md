# One Last Signal
A playable, original NASA Space Apps 2026 space-mission learning game. Career mode contains six chapters and eighteen progressively harder assignments, plus the original lunar spacecraft-design sandbox.

## Run
Node 22+, `npm install`, `npm run dev`. Production: `npm run build`, `npm run preview`. Tests: `npm test`.

## Play
Begin level 1, read the objective, then build the spacecraft and rocket in the mission workshop. Pop-up walkthroughs highlight the controls and pause the simulation while you read. The first satellite has a contact deadline and limited energy: transmitting before opening the solar wings can fail the mission. Win to unlock the next assignment. The Lunar sandbox opens the original detailed science mission, with selectable instruments/orbit, manual operations, report export, and local design saves.

## Scope and assumptions
This prototype uses circular lunar orbits and spherical occultation with fixed Earth/Sun directions over six representative orbits. Earth launch is an interactive two-stage ascent mini-game (4× time compression) with thrust, fuel, gravity, simple drag, vertical/lateral motion and a training steering corridor. Reaching 100 km does not establish orbit. Transfers remain illustrated and abstracted. Career insertion is a separate interactive timing/direction/Δv exercise; its low-point estimate interpolates a contract target rather than solving orbital mechanics. Sandbox insertion remains guided. Pointing modes in the lunar sandbox model a simplified body-fixed spacecraft with an eight-second slew. Data values are MiB internally (not RF Mbps); batteries use Wh. Packet reception is acknowledged immediately; science credits unique paired proxy/context records. Source constants are factual; costs, components, synthetic readings, footprint and target cells are educational approximations. No real ice detection is claimed. No NASA endorsement.

## Flight school update
The opening screen offers a seven-step hands-on walkthrough; use **Flight school** in the header to restart it. Steps require actual hardware selections, deployment, acquisition, received science and charging. Training coasts through modeled time to the first target window and pauses at learning checkpoints. After training, manual commands resume time and override the auto operator. The live orbital schematic explains target/Earth geometry. **Coast to window** advances resources normally and pauses on arrival; choose a command to continue. **Prioritize discoveries** schedules unseen measurements before repeats without discarding packets. Science score rewards coverage and received image detail, with a documented cap. Reception milestones and end-of-mission medals use actual state.

Additional verification: `node tests/tutorial.mjs` covers the guided mission, manual override, coast and mobile overflow. Browser scripts use the existing local Chromium executable and should be adjusted on other machines.

## Rocket ride and beginner introduction
Mission basics now explains the robot, rocket, equipment, orbit and win condition before flight school starts. After design review, Go to launchpad opens a 3D Earth launch with a four-second countdown, exhaust/smoke, clouds, fading sky, booster separation and engine audio (opt-in). Flight assistance holds at empty-booster separation; press Space or Separate booster to continue. Manual throttle and A/D steering affect acceleration/fuel use. The ascent mini-game ends only after the second engine burns and the vehicle reaches 100 km while climbing. A separate guided Moon-travel sequence explains coast and arrival before the explorer begins its existing operations mission.

## Career mode: 6 chapters, 18 assignments
Each chapter has Training, Operations and Mastery assignments with multiple goals. The path is Satellite -> Earth orbit -> Station assembly/docking -> Moon orbit -> Moon landing -> Mars survey and landing. Harder assignments change actual parameters: moving antenna alignment, low battery/shadow, more required survey areas, smaller storage/battery reserves, narrower docking ports, slower permitted contact speeds, smaller landing zones and reduced fuel. Mars uses weaker illustrative solar/downlink factors and combines an orbital survey with powered descent.

Win conditions are evaluated by the mission models. Successful assignments unlock the next. Stars rate performance; XP increases only for first completion or a higher mastery rating, preventing replay farming. Progress and the reusable launch certificate are saved locally under ols-career-v1. No server or global-competitive verification is claimed. The Lunar sandbox button opens the original detailed builder/tutorial.

The career orbital scenes are analytic 2D schematics, station docking is a simplified local relative-motion exercise, and Moon/Mars landing is a local powered-descent training model. These are playable skill challenges, not full KSP-style orbital rendezvous, atmospheric entry, surface terrain or station construction engines. Launch and transfer retain their documented approximations.

Additional factual constants: JPL https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/astro_par.html (Earth GM; Mars-system GM used as a spherical Mars approximation) and https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/phys_par.html (mean radii, rounded surface gravity). All mission equipment, XP, synthetic readings, communication rates and balance coefficients are game design inputs.

Tests: npm test covers progression, replay rewards, persistence, resource conservation and completion of every orbital/docking/landing tier under an explicit tested policy. node tests/career-browser.mjs covers first launch and two real career assignments plus reload/mobile checks. node tests/career-maneuvers.mjs uses documented seeded progress fixtures to exercise station docking, Moon descent and the combined Mars mission; fixture unlocks are test setup, not evidence a human completed the entire career.

## Mission workshop, failure and popup walkthroughs
Every career assignment now goes through a fixed-slot hardware builder: solar panels, battery, radio, instrument and recorder where relevant; booster, upper stage and propellant for the launch vehicle. Selected hardware changes actual mission power, energy, data production/downlink, dry mass, propellant, launch thrust and cost. Preflight blocks over-budget designs, overloaded delivery services and Mars missions without a deep-space stage. Passing review does not guarantee successful operation. Hardware figures, costs, launcher capacity and balance coefficients are fictional training specifications, not engineering estimates.

Satellite assignments have 60/52/44-second contact windows, battery depletion, antenna alignment, and a low-battery shadow/tracking challenge. The clock pauses in popup guides, but tips do not select equipment, press controls, complete objectives, or award XP. Failed attempts award no progress and can retry or return to the workshop. Performance rewards battery reserve and timely return; all three satellite tiers have tested feasible three-star policies. Launch, docking and landing start with assistance off in career; assisted docking/landing is capped at 79/100, below three-star mastery. The launch certificate skips only the repeated launch after a design passes preflight.

Popup walkthroughs open on first use and can be replayed from the workshop, launch and operations panels. They highlight controls, support keyboard focus trapping/Escape, and remember completion locally. The original sandbox flight-school tutorial remains available.

Verification: 40 model tests (`npm test`); browser checks cover real failures without unlocks, retry/success, popup clock pause, workshop constraints, manual launch, saved progress, later mission completion and mobile layouts. Run `node tests/career-browser.mjs`, `node tests/career-maneuvers.mjs`, `node tests/operations-browser.mjs`, `node tests/launch-mobile.mjs`, and `node tests/tutorial.mjs` against the dev server. These are deterministic training models, not a full freeform KSP construction or flight simulator.

## Playable flight stages
Every career assignment requires payload commissioning after launch or certificate delivery. Jettison the fairing, apply directional reaction-control torque, brake angular momentum, align the craft, and release it. Unsafe separation fails. From the Earth-orbit chapter onward, a manual insertion exercise requires prograde alignment, burning inside the indicated window, and cutting the engine in a target Δv band. Wrong direction, fuel waste, missed timing and excessive burn can fail. These are control exercises with explicit game contracts, not high-fidelity trajectory propagation.

Moon and Mars landings now lead to a rover expedition. Drive with WASD/arrow keys or the touch controls, avoid boulder fields, stop to collect 2/3/4 unique geological samples, manage power and shadow, return to the lander, then downlink. Driving cancels sampling; samples alone are insufficient until their evidence is received. Terrain, samples, speeds and power scaling are fictional. The rover's recharge/downlink rates depend on selected hardware. Rover damage, depleted battery and the deadline are genuine failure paths.

Mission XP is awarded only after every stage succeeds. The final rating averages completed stage ratings; a failed stage retries from its checkpoint, retaining earlier stage results. Redesign restarts the flight stages. The launch certificate skips only the ascent, never the new operations. A live stage strip makes the remaining tasks visible.

Launch now shows aerodynamic load, structural integrity, lateral speed and altitude-dependent lean limits. Sustained departure from the corridor causes loss after a recoverable warning; excessive aerodynamic loading can damage the rocket, and insufficient pad thrust fails explicitly. Lean limits define this vertical-ascent training contract, not a universal safety rule for real rockets. A/D and the direct lean slider steer; W/S changes throttle, X cuts thrust and Z restores full thrust. A regression reproduces the reported 65° lean at 47.9 km and verifies both loss and recovery policies.
